# Judge Q&A sheet

Twelve questions a judge is likely to ask. Every number comes from `README.md` or `results/` (seed 2026, 5,000 futures each, 2026 to 2076). Possible futures, not predictions.

## 1. Is this a prediction?
No. It is a policy flight simulator, not a climate model: the anchors are real (IPCC AR6 pathways, UN WPP 2024) but the mechanics are simple enough to read in one file. Every number we show is one possible future out of 5,000.

## 2. Where's the AI?
"Let AI decide" is a real optimizer, not a scripted answer. It does a random search plus hill-climbing over mixes of the 360-point budget, compares every candidate on the same seeds, and maximises the typical outcome plus half the worst-5% outcome. Claude is a third player: it chooses its own levers through the `simulate_future` MCP tool.

## 3. Why did the AI ignore coastal protection?
It put 1 point into coastal and heat adaptation because that lever moved the outcomes least per point. Adaptation doesn't lower emissions, it only reduces harm. So the AI kept 92% of futures below 2 °C, but in its most likely future 31 million people are displaced by the sea.

## 4. Why does Claude choose differently?
Claude picked a balanced mix: energy 80, carbon price 70, tech 70, cooperation 60, adaptation 50, food 30. Its stated reason was to decarbonize first while shielding people from warming already locked in. It lands in the middle: 26% of futures below 2 °C and 18 million displaced in its most likely future.

## 5. How do you know the numbers are right?
Everything is generated by `node scripts/results.mjs` into `results/`, and the engine has 11 tests (`npm test`). Each figure in the pitch is tied to a quote in `results/` or the README in a fact ledger that code checks. The model has limits, stated in the README: the anchors are rounded approximations.

## 6. What would you build next?
Put it in front of people: host the globe so anyone can try it on a phone (the phone layout is ready), and take it to schools and city halls with the funding programme. The ask is to let people take the sliders and try to beat the AI.

## 7. Who would use this?
People who debate letting AI run governments but have never seen the odds: young people who will be alive in 2076, schools and city halls. It lets them see where a choice leads before anyone decides.

## 8. How is this different from climate games?
It starts from an open argument, whether AI should decide, and gives the AI and the audience the same 360-point budget. Then it compares them on the same 5,000 futures: AI, Claude and audience side by side. The warming and sea-level pathways follow IPCC AR6 and population follows UN WPP 2024.

## 9. What did the audience do differently?
A sample people-first mix put 100 points into coastal adaptation. Only 8 million were displaced in its most likely future, but 0% of its futures stayed below 2 °C and median warming reached 2.98 °C.

## 10. Is Claude's answer consistent with its own claim?
Nearly. Claude's own line says 1.96 °C and about 17.5 million displaced for its single most-likely run. Across all 5,000 futures the median is 2.11 °C with 26% below 2 °C. We note this in `results/claude-in-charge.txt`.

## 11. What's the single takeaway?
The AI saved the climate and forgot the coast. It is the tension between the AI's 92% below 2 °C and the audience's 8 million displaced.

## 12. What are the weak spots of the model?
It is simple on purpose. Climate sensitivity varies per run (log-normal, sigma 0.18) with yearly noise of 0.05 °C, and nine event types such as pandemics, wars and breakthroughs are simplified. Say that on stage before a judge does.
